Reflection Questions

Answer the following questions in English based on the comic story above.

1 What communication problems are evident in the situation?

The main communication problems identified are:

  • Language Barrier: The investor speaks only English while the office staff primarily speaks Spanish. This creates a complete breakdown in verbal communication.
  • Lack of Preparedness: The company knew an international investor was coming but did not arrange for an English-speaking representative or translator in advance.
  • Non-verbal Misunderstanding: Carlos tried to use hand gestures and broken English, which led to confusion rather than clarity.
  • Professional Image Damage: The inability to communicate made the company appear unprofessional and unprepared for international business dealings.
  • Time Pressure: The investor had limited time, and the communication delay nearly cost the company a significant investment opportunity.
2 How does this affect the company's image?

The impact on the company's image is significant:

  • Unprofessional Perception: The investor perceived the company as disorganized and unprofessional, which is a major red flag for potential investors.
  • Lack of Global Readiness: In today's globalized business world, not having basic English communication capabilities signals that the company is not ready for international markets.
  • Loss of Trust: The investor questioned whether he could trust this company with his money if they cannot even handle a simple meeting.
  • Competitive Disadvantage: Other companies that are bilingual or have translation resources would appear much more attractive to international investors.
  • Reputation Risk: Word spreads quickly in business circles. The investor might share this negative experience with other potential partners.
3 What simple English phrases could have solved the problem immediately?

These simple phrases could have prevented the crisis:

"Please wait a moment"
Por favor espere un momento
"I will get someone who speaks English"
Traere alguien que hable ingles
"Would you like some coffee?"
Le gustaria un cafe?
"The manager will be here soon"
El gerente estara aqui pronto
"Let me use a translator"
Permitame usar un traductor
"Welcome to our company!"
Bienvenido a nuestra empresa!
4 Have you ever experienced a similar situation? Share your experiences.

Sample reflection for students:

"Yes, I have experienced a similar situation. Last year, I was working at a customer service desk when a tourist from France came in asking for directions. I only knew a few words in French, so I felt very embarrassed and frustrated. I tried to use Google Translate on my phone, but the connection was poor. Eventually, I drew a simple map on paper, which helped a little. This experience taught me that learning basic phrases in other languages is extremely valuable, and that technology can be a great help but should not be the only resource we rely on. Since then, I have been practicing English every day."

Key lessons from such experiences:

  • Always be prepared with basic phrases in common business languages.
  • Technology (translation apps) can be a lifesaver in emergency situations.
  • Body language and visual aids can bridge gaps when words fail.
  • Staying calm and showing willingness to help goes a long way.
  • Continuous language learning is an investment in your professional future.

Zero & First Conditional

A quick reference on how each conditional is formed, a video explanation, and real-life example sentences.

Zero Conditional

If + Present Simple, + Present Simple

Used for general truths, facts, habits, and things that always happen under a certain condition. Both clauses use the present simple tense, and the order of the clauses can be reversed without changing the meaning.

Example: If you heat ice, it melts.

First Conditional

If + Present Simple, + will + Base Verb

Used for real and possible situations in the future. The "if" clause describes a likely condition, and the result clause uses "will" to describe what will probably happen as a consequence.

Example: If it rains tomorrow, I will stay home.
